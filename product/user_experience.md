# Making friends is about finding multiple things in common

Someone is more interesting than the general public when you have two things you care about in common. As you discover more, you build the friendship. So, we want to make it easy to discover points of commonality in identity and interests, and to see the same folks over and over (the ones you want to see) so you discover more interests you have in common.

Some of this can be done by giving folks room to personalize who they are - self descriptions on the profile page, etc. Some by what folks choose to post. When we think about our "algorithm", we want to focus on helping folks find their hyperniche community online to be their online besties.

It's not just that you want to find other Heated Rivalry fans. It's that, of those Heated Rivalry fans, you will become casual friends with a handful or maybe 10-20 who share several interests and intersection points. But also, hopefully, you also find the people who share, like 10-15 points of intersection and become your online besties.

It's not that we're trying to show you a post that you might like, so much as the posts of people with things in common with you.

# Centering on Fandom when we think about the audience

We will purposefully choose to NOT have a global "for you" page. Discoverability will be through fandoms, tags, moots, or similar fandoms. We don't want to be maximizing engagement. We want to be maximizing connection and fandom joy.

Part of this choice comes from wanting to create a space where you are talking to people you feel like you know, rather than shouting at strangers. We don't want to be a yelling space about politics, or rigid opinions. Instead, we want to cultivate a space where it feels like you're talking with people you come to know and care about, and which is grounded, first, foremost, and always, in fandom.

We want to make purity/loyalty tests nonsensical rather than banned. I don't want to be in a space with people yelling"unfollow me if you ______". Sometimes, I might agree, sometimes, I might feel hurt and targeted by it. But it's just not the space we want. We want people to feel like they are opting in and filtering in people they want to talk to, rather than having to aggressively filter out strangers. We want to make it feel out of place to yell to an imagined crowd, because it feels like you are talking to people you "know", not yelling at strangers who might be watching.
